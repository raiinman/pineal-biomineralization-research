# Phase 1 Archive — Haritaki / Triphala

**Date:** August 15, 2026

## Trigger

A yogi / traditional-health claim proposed **Haritaki (Terminalia chebula)** as a substance that could “shatter” pineal calcification, chelate fluoride, or restore pineal function.

Rather than accept or dismiss the claim wholesale, the project separated three evidence lanes.

## Lane A — Historical / traditional

Haritaki and Triphala have longstanding Ayurvedic / Rasāyana use.

This establishes **historical use**, not modern pineal decalcification.

## Lane B — Real chemistry

Terminalia chebula contains tannin/polyphenol constituents including:
- chebulagic acid;
- chebulinic acid;
- gallic acid;
- related phenolics.

Relevant non-pineal observations include:
- metal-binding / chelation behavior;
- antioxidant chemistry;
- effects on nucleation/growth of some calcium-containing crystals in other systems.

## Lane C — Pineal-specific claim

Not established:
- direct dissolution of pineal hydroxyapatite;
- selective delivery to the pineal/acervulus interface;
- removal of pineal fluoride;
- regression of human pineal calcification;
- restoration of function through mineral removal.

## Scientific consequence

Haritaki remained useful because it helped force a cleaner testable question:

> Rather than asking whether “Haritaki decalcifies the pineal,” do purified constituents alter the dissolution, nucleation, or surface chemistry of **pineal-like carbonated hydroxyapatite** ex vivo?

This is a materials-science question and must remain separate from treatment claims.

## Project status

**INDIRECT / SIDEBAR CANDIDATE.**

Haritaki was not discarded, but it was demoted from “possible pineal tonic” to “compound family worth clean ex-vivo testing if the chemistry warrants it.”
