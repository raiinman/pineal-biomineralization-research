# Carbonated Hydroxyapatite / Biological Apatite

## Evidence state
Classic human pineal acervuli are strongly supported as calcium-phosphate biomineral broadly consistent with biological apatite / carbonate-containing hydroxyapatite, while other pineal calcification compartments may differ.

## Why this matters
The project originally treated pineal calcification as generic “calcium.” That was chemically inadequate.

Apatite-specific chemistry forces separation between:
- chelation of free calcium
- dissolution of mature mineral
- inhibition of nucleation
- inhibition of crystal growth
- surface passivation
- ion substitution and crystallinity changes

## Current physical model
Mature acervuli are heterogeneous, laminated mineral-organic composites rather than uniform inert crystals.

Relevant variables include:
- Ca²⁺ activity
- phosphate activity/speciation
- pH
- bicarbonate/carbonate
- Mg and fluoride substitution
- citrate/organic ligands
- surface-bound matrix
- cracks, pores, and diffusion paths
