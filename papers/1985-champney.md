# Champney et al. — 1985

> **Repository role:** Primary-paper dossier for the central adult-SCGx observation that established pineal concretion burden can fall dramatically.

**Title:** Superior cervical ganglionectomy results in the loss of pineal concretions in the adult male gerbil (Meriones unguiculatus)  
**Authors:** Thomas H. Champney, Bhaskar N. Joshi, Mary K. Vaughan, Russell J. Reiter  
**Journal:** The Anatomical Record 211(4):465–468  
**DOI:** 10.1002/ar.1092110414  
**PMID:** 3993997

## Study role
The central **adult established-concretion regression phenotype** in the project.

## Directly established from accessible primary record
- adult male gerbils
- bilateral superior cervical ganglionectomy
- 12-week post-operative endpoint
- number of pineal concretions drastically decreased
- effect reported irrespective of photoperiod treatment
- authors interpreted an active pineal state as necessary to maintain the normally high concretion burden

## Strong inference
Because the animals were adults, the study is evidence that a previously established concretion population can be lost/reduced after a physiological intervention.

## Not established
- direct serial tracking of individual concretions
- chemical dissolution kinetics
- total Ca/P mass balance
- route of mineral exit
- microglial uptake
- specific ECM protein changes

## Replication status
**No exact independent adult-SCGx regression replication has yet been located.**
